Before You StartWhat This Book Is

Functional Programming in Scala by Paul Chiusano and Rúnar Bjarnason (Manning; second edition with Michael Pilquist, updated for Scala 3) teaches FP by having you derive everything yourself: you write List, Option, a parallelism library, a test framework, a parser library, and then notice they all share the same shapes. That arc is what makes the book worth reading, and it's the arc this companion follows.

This is not a copy of the book. It's my own walk-through of its concepts, in my own words and my own code, written to be read on an iPad before or after the matching chapter. When the book gives you an exercise and moves on, this companion slows down: why the exercise exists, what the type signature is telling you, where people get stuck, and what the idea looks like if you've spent years in C++.

Functional programming is not a style of writing code. It's one constraint: write programs out of pure functions. Everything else in this book (immutable data, Option, monads, IO) is what you discover you need once you take that one constraint seriously.

How To Read ItThe Rhythm of a Chapter

Every chapter has the same parts:

  • The problem first. Each idea is introduced by something that hurts without it.
  • Step-by-step derivation. Small code blocks, each one change from the last. Read them in order.
  • Boxes. Analogy for intuition, Coming from C++ for the mapping to what you already know, Hold onto this for the one sentence to remember, Things that bite for common mistakes.
  • Exercises. Try each one before opening the solution. In FP the struggle with the types is the learning.
  • Recap and jargon decoder at the end, for review.

ContentsFifteen Chapters, Four Parts

Part I (Ch. 1–6) gives you the basics: pure functions and the core data types. Part II (Ch. 7–9) designs three real libraries the functional way. Part III (Ch. 10–12) pulls the shared structure out of those libraries: monoids, monads, applicatives. Part IV (Ch. 13–14) handles the outside world: I/O, mutation and streams.

PacingA Realistic Plan

At about an hour a day: Part I takes two weeks. Do every exercise there, because the rest of the book assumes it's automatic. Part II takes two weeks. Read for the design process, not the specific library. Part III takes a week and a half and is where it clicks. Part IV takes a week. If you're short on time, read 1 → 3 → 4 → 6 → 10 → 11 → 13. That path covers the core ideas you'll use in real Scala codebases with cats-effect or ZIO.
